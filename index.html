IOT Final Year Project

FloodGuard AI

IoT flood early-warning system

Flash floods often give communities too little warning to get to safety in time.

A LoRa sensor network (923 MHz, Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V3) with an HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor and YL-83 rain sensor, reporting to a Flask + WebSocket dashboard with live water level, rainfall, and risk status. A Random Forest classifier sorts flood risk into Low/Medium/High, beating a threshold baseline by 17 points. Every reading is logged to a SQLite database queried live for the dashboard and event log, with one-click Excel export (10 min/1 hr/24 hr/all-time) for reporting and analysis. Across a 144-day field deployment: 99.0% accuracy on the held-out test set, 92.9% on 420 real sensor readings, 100% LoRa packet delivery indoors, and 1.6 cm sensor MAE against reference measurements.

Smart Agro-Sentinel

AI tomato leaf disease detection ("Doctor Plants")

A tomato crop can be lost before a farmer notices what's wrong with it.

An ESP32-CAM and push button capture a leaf image, which a TensorFlow CNN classifies for early blight, late blight, curl virus, and bacterial spot. Results reach a Blynk mobile app and a Flask-based web dashboard with disease name, confidence score, and care recommendations, plus non-leaf image rejection and CSV-exportable prediction history. Average model confidence ran 0.83–0.97 across disease classes over the test images.

RF Simulation

3.6 GHz LNA for 5G Receivers

Low-noise amplifier design

A 5G receiver is only as good as the weak, noisy signal it starts with.

A 3.6 GHz low-noise amplifier built around a Qorvo QPL9057 transistor on Rogers RT/duroid 5880, matched via Smith chart and transformed from lumped elements into a distributed microstrip layout in Keysight Genesys. The optimized design reached a forward gain of 15.2 dB and a noise figure of 1.64 dB, with stable operation (K just above unity) at the target frequency.

3.5 GHz Interdigital Bandpass Filter

5G filter design, fabrication & VNA testing

A receiver needs to hear its own band clearly and ignore everything crowding around it.

A ninth-order 0.2 dB Chebyshev interdigital bandpass filter at 3.5 GHz, hand-calculated from the low-pass prototype through frequency transformation to nine coupled microstrip resonators on FR4, then manually tuned in Genesys until the passband hit all seven required ripples. Milled onto a physical FR4 board and measured on a Rohde & Schwarz vector network analyzer, with the measured response closely tracking simulation around 3.5 GHz.
